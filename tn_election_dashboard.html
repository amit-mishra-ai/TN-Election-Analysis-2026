Flip matrix — from 2021 winner → 2026 winner (flipped seats only)
The dominant pattern: 65 DMK seats flipped directly to TVK — the single largest seat transfer in this election. Combined with AIADMK's 26 seats, TVK collected 104 flips total, accounting for 63.8% of all flipped seats.

AIADMK's paradox: While losing 26 to TVK, AIADMK gained 22 from DMK and 2 from PMK — ending up as the net-second-largest gainer of flipped seats (net −7 on flips), suggesting a consolidation of anti-DMK vote where TVK didn't dominate.

Vote source attribution model — where did TVK's 35.1% come from?
TVK vote sources: ~41.5% from DMK defection, ~35.4% from AIADMK defection, ~19.1% new voters, ~4% others.
Both rivals bled to TVK, but DMK more so. The correlation between TVK vote share and DMK's decline (r=0.37) is stronger than with AIADMK's decline (r=0.27), suggesting TVK pulled more from the DMK ecosystem — likely younger urban voters, first-time voters mobilised by Vijay's celebrity, and disaffected DMK cadres frustrated with governance.

New voters matter. 2026 saw 6.7 million more votes cast versus 2021 — a ~14.5% increase. TVK's 35.1% state-wide share applied to this larger base means a significant portion of TVK votes came from previously non-voting or newly eligible populations. Chennai Metro, where TVK hit 46.9%, had a particularly large urban youth influx.

Regional nuance: In Kongu, AIADMK's vote share fell 15.9pp while DMK's fell only 8.9pp — TVK likely drew more from AIADMK in this traditionally AIADMK-dominant belt. In Chennai Metro, DMK's collapse (−21.3pp) vs AIADMK's smaller drop (−12.6pp) confirms TVK as the successor to DMK's urban base in the capital.
